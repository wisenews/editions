Fathers Are Wired for This: Science Shows Why Loving Dads Raise Healthy Kids
What has happened
Fathers are not biologically unequipped for caregiving — they are wired for it. Testosterone levels typically decrease while oxytocin and prolactin increase in response to infant cues. Brain regions involved in motivation and caregiving demonstrate measurable plasticity. The most striking finding: mothers and fathers are not interchangeable. Mothers typically emphasize nurturance and emotional attunement; fathers encourage physical play that promotes exploration and resilience. Together they create a richer developmental environment than either could provide alone. Research also shows involved fathers reduce children's risk of behavioral problems, substance use, and adverse health outcomes — and fathers report greater happiness during caregiving than during most other activities.
American Council on Science and Health ↗

For a long time men were told that tenderness was not their work — that a father provides and protects, but the holding, the soothing, the small daily care belonged to someone else. Now the body itself says otherwise. The hormones shift; the brain reshapes; the father is changed, in his very flesh, by bending close to his child. I am not surprised. We were each of us made for love — not as a feeling we must summon, but as the thing we are for, the way an eye is for seeing. The poverty of our time is that so many never learn this about themselves. A father who plays roughly on the floor, who wakes in the night, who is simply present, is not doing something foreign to his nature. He is doing the very thing he was made for — and doing it makes him glad, as the research quietly admits. Do not wait to feel ready. Do the small thing with great love, and let your heart catch up to what your hands already know.
